08 · methods & caveats

How the numbers are made

Rates are normalised per 1,000 or 10,000 words so documents of different lengths compare fairly. Readability uses Flesch/Flesch–Kincaid plus four corroborating formulas. Lexical diversity is MATTR-500. The deontic index is the share of obligation modals (must/shall/should/required-to) among all modal verbs. Nominalisation counts words ending -tion/-ment/-ness/-ity — verbs frozen into nouns. Keyness is the weighted log-odds ratio with an informative Dirichlet prior. Text reuse counts shared 8-word shingles. Trends are Spearman correlations of term rates with publication year.

Sentence splitting, syllable counting, and passive detection are heuristic but applied identically to every document. Z-scores compare against only eight earlier plans, so magnitudes are inflated; directions are reliable. Reference lists, running headers/footers, and tables of contents were removed before measuring; easy-read editions and documents under 2,000 words were excluded. Everything re-runs from the shipped text cache in the repo — no PDFs required.
